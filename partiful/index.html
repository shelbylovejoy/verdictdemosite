Why I'd build it

The problem, what's already there, how I'd test it, and why me.

01

Maybe is where plans go to wait

Maybe is useful for about a day. After that it's a placeholder nobody comes back to, and the host is left guessing how much to buy.

The other half is saying no. Declining where the whole guest list can see it feels like a statement, so people stay on maybe or go quiet. Partiful already made inviting people easy, with a link that opens from a text and no app. This is the other end of the same flow.

02

The pieces are already there

  • A 2024 post called “things i hate about partiful” calls out maybes that never get resolved and decline messages that feel loaded. Those are the two things this changes.

    Substack, May 2024

  • Date polls already work this way. Once the host picks a date, everyone's RSVP updates on its own. Lock-in applies the same idea to the RSVP itself.

    Partiful on the App Store

  • Partiful runs on texts. Guests only need a verified phone number to RSVP, so one well-timed text fits how people already use it.

    Wikipedia
Research
03

Try it on bigger parties first

I'd turn it on for events with 15 or more invites, where a wrong headcount actually costs something, and compare them with similar events that don't have it.

Measure

  • Share of maybes answered before the event
  • Host's headcount vs. who came, from a one-tap check after

Watch

  • Not this time vs. silent no-shows
  • Hosts who throw another party within 60 days

Guardrails

  • Going count doesn't drop
  • Text opt-outs don't rise
Experiment design
04

I design for the people who didn't make the plan

At Paramount I built Verdict, a game that gets a room to agree on a movie. The host has the TV and the subscription. Everyone else just has a phone, so I designed for the guest first: they join from the browser, with no app and no account.
