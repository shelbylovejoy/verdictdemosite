Hike as a group, not a line.

Group hikes go wrong in two places: picking a route everyone can manage, and losing each other on the way. Hiiker already has the maps, the off-route alerts and a live locator. It could handle both.

Why I'd build it

The problem, what's already there, how I'd test it, and why me.

01

Groups split up on the trail

The person who plans a group hike picks a route for the whole group and hopes everyone can do it. On the day, the group stretches out, and the slowest person ends up alone at the back.

Hiiker is built for navigation, and it's very good at it. A group layer would turn the same maps into a way to keep everyone together.

Problem framing
17%
of mountain rescue callouts in England and Wales in 2024 were to look for a missing person. 18 to 24 year olds were the most rescued age group (Mountain Rescue England and Wales, via MBR).
02

Why I think it works

  • Hiiker already has off-route alerts and a live locator, and a 2026 TGO review named it Best in Test. The group map builds on parts that already exist.

    TGO Magazine, Jun 2026

  • In 2024, mountain rescue teams in England and Wales had 3,842 callouts. 17% were searches for a missing person and 8% were people who reported themselves lost.

    MBR, Apr 2025

  • Every group hike brings new people into the app. Hiiker grew to around 600,000 users with a team of 12 and is pushing into the UK, Europe and the US, so organizers who invite friends are a cheap growth loop.

    Business Post
Research
03

Start with organizers who already plan routes

People who plan routes in Hiiker and then share them are the natural first organizers. I'd give them group hikes and compare with organizers who keep sharing a GPX file.

Measure

  • Group hikes created
  • Share of invited hikers who vote

Watch

  • Invitees who install Hiiker
  • Organizers who upgrade

Guardrails

  • Battery use during a group hike
  • Location sharing ends at the trailhead
Experiment design
04

I've built group decisions on phones

At Paramount I built Verdict, a game where everyone votes from their own phone and the group lands on one answer. Picking a route is the same kind of decision, with higher stakes.
