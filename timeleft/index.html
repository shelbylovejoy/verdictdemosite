Make the second dinner happen.

Timeleft is great at getting six strangers to one table. What happens next depends on whoever's brave enough to ask for a number. If several people at a table want to meet again, Timeleft could just book it.

Why I'd build it

The problem, what's already there, how I'd test it, and why me.

01

The best tables end with a goodbye

A table clicks, everyone says they should do this again, and then nobody organizes it. One writer who went to a Timeleft dinner described follow-up invitations coming from a fellow diner, not from the app.

Timeleft already asks people who they'd like to see again. The step from that answer to an actual second dinner is still left to the guests.

Problem framing
1 in 6
people worldwide are affected by loneliness, according to the World Health Organization (2025). One good dinner is a start. A second one is where a friendship can start.
02

Timeleft already has the signal

  • After dinner, guests can already choose who they'd like to see again, and rate the restaurant.

    The Brussels Times, Aug 2024

  • There's no official follow-up today. Guests swap contacts on their own, and invitations come from diners rather than the platform.

    Alta Journal, Feb 2025

  • The scale makes it worth doing. More than 11,500 people sit down each Wednesday, in over 300 cities across 60 countries.

    Alta Journal, Feb 2025

  • Loneliness is the problem Timeleft exists to fix, and it's widespread: about 1 in 6 people worldwide, and 17 to 21% of 13- to 29-year-olds.

    World Health Organization, Jun 2025
Research
03

Start in the busiest cities

Cities with the most Wednesday tables will produce mutual groups fastest. I'd offer second dinners there and compare those diners with similar ones who don't get the offer.

Measure

  • Tables that produce a mutual group
  • Second dinners booked from them

Watch

  • 60-day retention for people who had a second dinner
  • Subscription upgrades

Guardrails

  • Regular dinners still mix in new people
  • Nobody can tell who didn't pick them
Experiment design
04

I design for the awkward part

At Paramount I built Verdict, a game that gets a group to one answer without anyone having to say it out loud. That's the hard part of “we should do this again,” too.
