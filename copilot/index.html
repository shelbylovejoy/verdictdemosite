Why I'd build it

The problem, what's already there, how I'd test it, and why me.

01

Sharing today means sharing everything

Couples and roommates split rent, groceries and a few cards. They don't want to merge their whole financial lives to do it.

Copilot's current answer is to sign in on a second device with the same account, which gives that device full control. It works for some couples. For everyone else, it means either sharing too much or not using Copilot together at all.

Problem framing
1 login
is how sharing works today, and every device signed in gets full control of the account (Copilot help center).
02

People are already asking

  • “Couple/Family Support - Shared Budgets” is an open request on Copilot's own feedback board, with five related requests merged into it. The team has said there's no ETA yet.

    Copilot feedback board

  • The workaround is a Magic Link sign-in to the same account, and joint accounts have to be connected only once or transactions show up twice.

    Copilot help center

  • Reviews list no joint or couples access as a con next to Copilot's strengths in design and AI categorization.

    The Penny Hoarder, 2026
Research
03

Start with households already sharing a login

Households that already share one login are the ones who want this most. I'd offer them a proper invite first and compare them with similar households that keep sharing a login.

Measure

  • Households that switch to separate logins
  • Weekly active days per person

Watch

  • Cancellations from either partner
  • Annual plan renewals

Guardrails

  • Nothing private shows up in the household view
  • Categorization accuracy stays the same
Experiment design
