Why I'd build it

The problem, what's already there, how I'd test it, and why me.

01

A charging stop is dead time with a timer on it

Fast charging takes long enough to get boring and not long enough to start a movie. The car already knows how many minutes are left, and the people in it already have phones in their hands.

AirConsole's catalog fits that window well. What's missing is the car doing the math: which games fit the time left and the number of players, so nobody has to scroll a car screen and guess.

02

The timing is right

  • Volkswagen is bringing AirConsole to six 2027 models, including the ID. Buzz. Games run while the car is parked, phones join by scanning a QR code, and 20 games are available at launch.

    The Next Web, Sep 2026

  • VW pitched it as a way to turn everyday moments into shared ones. Charging is the everyday moment every EV owner has.

    The Next Web, Sep 2026

  • Paid fast-charging sessions at non-Tesla chargers average 42 minutes. Including Tesla, the average is 31.

    U.S. Department of Energy, Dec 2023 · Jalopnik, Jan 2024

  • It travels across brands. AirConsole also works with BMW, Audi, Porsche, Škoda and Tata, so one charging mode could ship everywhere.

    The Next Web, Sep 2026
Research
03

Start with the ID. Buzz

The ID. Buzz is electric and comes with a year of In-Vehicle Premium, so every owner can try it. I'd turn the charging mode on there first and compare charging stops with and without it.

Measure

  • Sessions started while charging
  • Sessions that finish before unplugging

Watch

  • In-Vehicle Premium renewals after the free year
  • Passengers who join with their phone

Guardrails

  • No play while driving
  • People don't stay plugged in longer just to finish a game
Experiment design
04

I've built this pattern before

At Paramount I built Verdict. The TV hosts, everyone scans a QR code, votes from their own phone, and nobody installs anything. AirConsole is the same pattern on a bigger set of screens, and I'd like to build it for cars.

I'm a CS student at Georgia Tech, and I'd like to spend next summer building at AirConsole.
