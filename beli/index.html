Why I'd build it

The problem, what's already there, how I'd test it, and why me.

01

Dinner plans die in the group chat

Every group chat has the same thread. Someone asks where we should eat, four people say they don't care, and the one person who does ends up deciding. Meanwhile the answer is sitting in their Beli lists.

Beli doesn't have messaging, so that thread happens somewhere else. The decision should happen where the lists are.

Problem framing
87%
of diners take a friend's recommendation more seriously than an online rating (TouchBistro survey of 521 U.S. diners, 2017). Beli is built on that.
02

Beli already has the hard parts

  • The founders have said they want to make it easier to plan dining experiences. This is one way to do that.

    Boston Globe, Aug 2026

  • Want to Try lists are already where plans start. You can save places straight from Instagram and TikTok posts.

    Food Network, Aug 2025

  • Booking is already built in through OpenTable and SevenRooms, so a match can turn into a reservation without leaving the app.

    The Wall Street Journal, May 2025

  • It's a young, social crowd. About 80% of users are under 35, and most join because a friend brought them in.

    Food Network, Aug 2025
Research
03

Start with dinners for four in New York

New York is where Beli is busiest, and four is about where a group chat stops agreeing on anything. I'd test it there first, then open it up.

Measure

  • Plans that end in a reservation
  • Minutes from starting a plan to a match

Watch

  • Rankings logged the day after
  • Friends who join Beli from a plan

Guardrails

  • Want to Try adds don't drop
  • Nobody's private lists get exposed
Experiment design
04

I've built group decisions before

At Paramount I built Verdict, a game that helps a room pick a movie. Everyone votes on their own phone, with no app and no account, and the TV shows what the group wants. It's the same problem: nobody wants to be the one who picks.
