Why I'd build it

The problem, what's already there, how I'd test it, and why me.

01

A watchlist is a list for one

Watchlists grow faster than anyone can watch them. In one r/Letterboxd thread, members describe watchlists of 1,700 and 2,000+ films, trimming a shortlist twice a week and still ending up with a full page, and picking by dice, a spinner wheel or a jar.

That's with one person. Add a second and the films you'd both say yes to are buried in two lists nobody can see side by side.

Problem framing
16 min
Average time viewers aged 18 to 34 spend browsing before they pick something (Gracenote, a Nielsen company, 2025). More than half of Letterboxd members are under 35.
02

Letterboxd already has most of the parts

  • Pro and Patron members can already filter by the streaming services they pay for. It just works for one person at a time.

    Wikipedia

  • Video Store launched in December 2025, so a match that isn't streaming anywhere can still be watched tonight.

    TechCrunch, Nov 2025

  • Members are already building workarounds, from shuffling the watchlist to third-party picker sites. A version inside Letterboxd keeps the night on Letterboxd, and it ends with two diary entries.

    r/Letterboxd

  • It's a big, young audience. Letterboxd passed 30 million members in 2026, and more than half are under 35.

    Wikipedia · Forbes Argentina, Sep 2026
Research
03

Start with couples and roommates

Two people is the simplest version, and it's the most common movie night. I'd launch it there, then open it to groups.

Measure

  • Film nights that end in a logged film within a day
  • Minutes from start to match

Watch

  • Pro upgrades that start from the shared-services filter
  • Video Store rentals that start from a match

Guardrails

  • Watchlist adds don't drop
  • Nobody sees films outside the overlap
Experiment design
04

This is the problem I already worked on

At Paramount I built Verdict, a game that helps a room pick a movie. Everyone swipes the same ten movies on their own phone, the TV shows the top three, and you tap the winner to start watching. I started from the same research on how long people spend deciding.

I'm a CS student at Georgia Tech, and I'd like to spend next summer building at Letterboxd.
