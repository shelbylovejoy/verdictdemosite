How it works

4 steps · Pasos
  1. 1

    Start. The host taps "Start a game" on the TV, and a QR code appears.

  2. 2

    Join. Everyone scans it, picks a nickname, and chooses up to three moods.

  3. 3

    Swipe. Everyone swipes the same 10 movies on their phone.

  4. 4

    Pick. The TV shows the top three. Tap the winner to start watching.

    If it's a tie, everyone ranks their top three in a final round.

My PM work

Seven things I did as the PM, in order.

01 Endorsement · EndosoPM skill: Problem framing

I found the problem

My friends and I once scrolled for a movie so long that it ruined the night. This happens a lot. U.S. viewers spend about 12 minutes deciding what to watch (Nielsen, 2025). The catalog wasn't the problem. One person holds the remote, and nobody wants to be the one who picks.

12 minDeciding what to watch
02 Endorsement · EndosoPM skill: User focus

I designed for the guest

The host has the Apple TV and the subscription. Everyone else just has a phone, and it might be an Android. So guests join from their browser, with no app and no account.

03 Endorsement · EndosoPM skill: Iteration

I shaped it with a VP of Product

I met six times with Paramount's VP of Product Management, Streaming Innovation. We talked through the overall design, where the game should live, and how people would actually use it. Those talks moved Verdict from Tinder-style swiping between phones to a Jackbox-style game on the TV.

04 Endorsement · EndosoPM skill: User research

I tested it with users

I ran a playtest with about eight interns and took notes by hand. Their biggest ask was picking by mood instead of genre, so I rebuilt the picker around moods. I also demoed it to senior leaders from several teams and a family at home.

"I can totally see this changing the way I browse content."
"I wish I had this for my last movie night."
05 Endorsement · EndosoPM skill: Prioritization

I cut the scope

I had six weeks, so I kept only what gets a room from "what should we watch?" to a pick. I said no to accounts, an iPhone-only app, and AI recommendations. Each one would have turned Verdict back into a regular streaming app.

06 Endorsement · EndosoPM skill: Metrics and risk

I defined success

Verdict didn't ship, so these are the numbers I'd launch with.

I also planned for what could break it. Spotty Wi-Fi falls back to the local network, ties go to a final round, and the game only stores a nickname, which is deleted when the game ends.

North star

Games where the winner starts playing within 5 minutes

Watch

Guardrail

07 Endorsement · EndosoPM skill: Communication

I pitched it to leadership

In my last week, I demoed Verdict to Paramount's SVP of Innovation and Emerging Platforms and the VP of Product Management, Streaming Innovation. They said it filled a real need and saw a market for it. They said I shouldn't be afraid if this ends up showing up on my screen one day. It was my last week, so the project ended there.

What I'd do differently

I built this a bit siloed. Next time, I'd do user research before I start building, so the idea isn't just living in my own head.

How I built it

SwiftUI on the Apple TV, a plain web page for phones, and Firebase to pass votes between them. I used Claude Code to build fast, but the product calls were mine. When it pushed an Apple-only design, I kept it open to Android, because most living rooms aren't all iPhones.
