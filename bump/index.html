Make the plan on the map.

Bump shows where your friends are and lights up when they're together. The step in between, deciding where to meet, still happens in a group chat. A plan pin could settle it on the map, then turn into a gathering when everyone shows up.

Why I'd build it

The problem, what's already there, how I'd test it, and why me.

01

Bump knows where. It doesn't help decide.

Bump's own description says it's there to help friends see what each other are doing and make plans to meet up. The seeing part is built: a live map, places you've been, and gatherings that light up when friends are together.

The planning part still lives in chat, where “where should we go?” can stall for an hour. A plan pin puts that question on the map, where everyone can already see how far each option is.

Problem framing

02

Bump already has the pieces

  • Bump says it's built to help friends see what each other are doing and make plans to meet up, with a live map, group chat from the map, and place search that shows which friends have been somewhere.

    Bump help center, May 2026

  • Gatherings already show a single pin when friends are together, with who's there, when they arrived, and a group chat. There's no step before it for deciding where to go.

    Bump help center

  • Young Americans have lost the most time with friends. Time spent socializing for ages 15 to 24 fell from about an hour a day to 35 minutes over 20 years.

    Axios, Jul 2026
Research
03

Start with groups that already chat in Bump

Friend groups that already message each other in Bump most days are planning somewhere. I'd give them plan pins and compare them with similar groups.

Measure

  • Plans dropped
  • Plans that turn into gatherings

Watch

  • Weekly active friend groups
  • Invites to friends not on Bump yet

Guardrails

  • Plans are only visible to invited friends
  • No change to who sees your location
Experiment design
04

I build for the group decision

At Paramount I built Verdict, where everyone votes from their own phone and the group lands on one answer quickly, so nobody gets stuck being the one who decides. Picking where to meet is the same decision with a map.
