Why I'd build it

The problem, what's already there, how I'd test it, and why me.

01

Shared editing isn't shared deciding

Group trips run on one planner. In a 2026 Klook survey, 72% of trip planners in the Philippines said their group's trips only happen because of them, and most spend more than ten hours planning.

Edit access doesn't change that. Adding something to a trip someone else built feels like overstepping, so tripmates stay quiet and the planner guesses.

02

Why I think it works

  • Wanderlog already has the group. More than a million people have tried it, and collaborators can work on the same trip for free. What's missing is a way to weigh in that takes two minutes.

    wanderlog.com

  • Google added voting on shared lists of places to Maps in 2018, so the gesture is already familiar. It just doesn't live inside an itinerary.

    Google, Sep 2018

  • Hearing about it after the trip is worse. In the same survey, 55.7% of planners said a common complaint is that another itinerary would have been better.

    Inquirer on Klook's survey, Aug 2026
Research
03

Test it on trips with three or more people

That's where one planner is doing the work for a group. I'd compare trips that use Ask the group against similar trips that don't.

Measure

  • Share of invited tripmates who vote
  • Itinerary stops that came from a vote

Watch

  • Tripmates who install the app after voting
  • Planners who upgrade to Pro

Guardrails

  • Time to a finished itinerary doesn't go up
  • Planners keep using it after the first try
Experiment design
04

I've built a group vote before

At Paramount I built Verdict, a game that helps a room pick a movie. Everyone votes from their own phone, with no app and no account, and the TV shows what the group wants. It's the same shape: a group that needs to decide, and one person who doesn't want to be the only one deciding.
