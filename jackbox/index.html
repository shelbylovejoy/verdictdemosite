Why I'd build it

The problem, what's already there, how I'd test it, and why me.

01

The pick is the slowest part of the night

Jackbox has made 62 games across 27 products, and Party Pack 12 adds five more this fall. Every new game makes the gap between games a little longer. Right now that gap belongs to the host. They back out to a menu, scroll, and guess what the room wants, while everyone else waits holding a phone that does nothing.

It's about to matter more. Jackbox's upcoming TV app is planned to offer the full catalog by subscription. When every game is one click away, choosing becomes part of the product.

02

Jackbox has been chipping at this for years

  • The Jackbox blog has guides to choosing games by player count and by category, and links to fan-made pickers. Those are the same filters the vote would apply on its own.

    Jackbox blog, player counts · categories

  • The Megapicker puts every pack in one launcher with sorting and filters. It's on Steam, and only the host touches it. Reviewers also ask for a random-pick button.

    Jackbox, July 2024 · Steam reviews, summarized by Vaporlens

  • The controllers are already in the room. The whole format rests on the fact that almost everyone has a phone.

    Mike Bilder, CEO, in Checkpoint Gaming, Oct 2025
Research
03

Ship it as a toggle and watch the gap

I'd start with an opt-in toggle in the TV app beta, where Jackbox controls the whole flow, then compare sessions with it against sessions without.

Measure

  • Seconds from the results screen to the next game starting
  • Games played per session

Watch

  • How often the host overrides the vote
  • Share of audience members who vote

Guardrails

  • Sessions don't get shorter
  • No new drop-off at the vote screen
Experiment design
04

I've built this mechanic once

At Paramount I built Verdict, a game where the TV hosts, everyone votes from their own phone, and the room picks a movie. It started as Tinder-style swiping between phones. After six sessions with a VP of Product, it became a Jackbox-style game on the TV.

I'm a CS student at Georgia Tech, and I'd like to spend next summer working on the real thing.
